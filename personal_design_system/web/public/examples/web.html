Spatial Vignettes

Designing arrival moments for mixed reality. A system that uses audio-first transitions to make entering a virtual space feel intentional, calm, and human.

The Problem

Teleportation feels wrong

Mixed reality environments load instantly. You tap a button and you are in a new room. But the human brain does not work that way. We expect transitions, thresholds, arrival. Without them, every space change feels like a jump cut in a film that needed a dissolve.

We set out to design a transition system that gives people the sensation of arriving somewhere, not being teleported. The constraint: each transition had to last under 12 seconds. It needed to be felt, not watched.

[image: early wireframes of the vignette system]

Approach

Audio before image

The key insight came from user research. When we tested visual-first transitions (the room appears, then sound fills in), participants rated the experience 3.1 out of 5 on desirability. When we reversed the order (ambient audio fades in, then the visual scene resolves), the score jumped to 4.2.

Sound prepares the brain. It sets spatial expectations before the eyes have to process a new environment. One participant described it as "walking into a room where someone already turned the lights on for you."

Design principle: the space should feel ready for you, not built in front of you. Audio is how we signal readiness.

We built five vignettes covering the most common spatial transitions in a workday: morning context, commute handoff, workspace entry, focus session, and wind-down. Each follows the same audio-first pattern but adapts duration and intensity to context.

Outcome

A reusable transition component

The vignette system ships as a single, parameterized component. Any spatial experience on the platform can call it with a context type, duration, and audio profile. The transition handles the rest: fade curves, spatial audio positioning, visual resolve timing.

Three of five vignettes reached high-fidelity prototype in the first sprint. The remaining two are pending platform API access for spatial audio positioning. Sprint closes June 6, 2026.
